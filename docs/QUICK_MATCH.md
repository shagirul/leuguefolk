# Opponent-first match entry

Tap the leaderboard add button to choose from the group, or hold it for 280 ms to open up to five suggested opponents. Glide over an avatar to enlarge it and reveal its name; release over that avatar to open score entry. Releasing elsewhere or cancelling the gesture does nothing. This gesture only selects the person: a match is submitted after the score is chosen and Send is pressed. A subsequent generated click is suppressed to prevent opening a second flow.

The keyboard alternative is Shift+Enter on the add button, arrow keys to highlight, Enter to choose, or Escape to cancel. Tapping a tray avatar also works.

The quick tray uses a compact dark pill with unframed character avatars and a lightly shaded screen behind it. The active avatar enlarges above the pill, and its name appears above the row. The shade and tray are removed together on dismissal or selection. Browser checks verified dismissal and the narrow-phone layout.

Matches opens on **Requests**, with Sent and Verified available as filters. Sent contains outgoing proposals, cancelled reports you raised, and locally queued new reports; Requests contains actions waiting on you. Compact conversation rows put the score under the name: green for an agreed win, muted red for a loss, and neutral for pending scores. Win/loss text accompanies color; status badges and trailing chevrons are omitted. The right edge shows time for today, Yesterday, a weekday for two to six days ago, then a date. Swipe a match row left to reveal the action for recording another match with that opponent; keyboard users can press Arrow Left to reveal it and Escape to close it. This opens score entry and never submits a match automatically.

Suggestions use verified matches and current group membership: last opponent first, then most frequent over the last 30 days, then favorites and remaining members, with stable name/ID ordering. Exclude the current player, cancelled/unverified reports, outsiders and future timestamps. Favorites are saved on this device, separately for each signed-in player and group. The normal picker shows all opponents; search appears at eight opponents, and its query survives a favorite toggle. Three enrolled players therefore produce exactly two choices without search.

Quick scoring explicitly labels the format **Singles · First to 11**. The reporter is the selected winner by default and may switch to the opponent. Choosing a losing score below 10 produces an 11-point winner; 10 or higher produces losing score + 2. For example: 9 → 11–9; 10 → 12–10; 13 → 15–13. The compact horizontal drum cycles through 0–198, respecting the existing 200-point server limit, and supports scrolling, tapping, left/right arrows, PageUp/PageDown and Home/End. No score is submitted until the player explicitly chooses a losing score. The exact two scores are shown before Send.

**Enter full score** keeps both numbers editable for other formats, house rules or unfinished games. Changing to a new opponent clears the score choice and restores the reporter as default winner. The server receives the same explicit score pair as before; existing mutual confirmation, corrections, offline queuing and version checks still apply. No database schema change is required.

Validation: unit tests cover score derivation/deuce, invalid inputs, suggestion priority, small/large groups, full scores, hold/glide/release, pointer cancellation, outside release and duplicate-click suppression. Browser checks used real Firebase demo emulators: two-choice picker, nine-player search, favorite persistence, five-avatar tray, winner switching, score cycling, full-score fallback, and submission of the calculated 10–12 pending report. Layout was checked at 390px and 320px. Browser interaction used the keyboard alternative for the quick tray; pointer handling is covered by the gesture tests. Touch behavior should also be tried on a physical phone during the office pilot.

Reference: [Meta's description of tap-and-hold sharing](https://about.fb.com/news/2022/03/introducing-new-instagram-messaging-features/), [Apple picker guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/pickers), [ITTF game-scoring rules, section 2.11](https://db.ittf.com/sites/default/files/public/2023-09/2023_ITTF_Statutes_clean_version2_2023-09-01_2.pdf).

The opponent summary row is removed from score entry. Change opponent is an icon inside the opponent winner tile (and beside the opponent name in full-score mode). The game format and Full score/Quick score switch share one line below the score controls. The horizontal dial is 76px tall, with exact score preview and the confirmation action visible at 320×640. The layout was visually checked with a long opponent name.

**Accept all** on Requests opens a frozen review of the exact incoming score proposals. Confirming persists separate versioned confirmations in the existing durable outbox. Changed requests are skipped, new arrivals are not added to the reviewed selection, duplicate queued actions are excluded, and account/group changes stop further queuing. Cancellations, disputes and verified-score amendments require individual review. Only successful server confirmations affect standings. A local storage error reports partial progress; it does not claim that unsaved confirmations were accepted. Unit tests and a real Firebase emulator workflow cover these cases.
